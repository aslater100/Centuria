# Centuria 2.0 — "A world that answers back" (scope)

## Context
The game feels unsatisfying even though the sim is deep. Three reasons:
- **Inputs vanish into systems.** The player calls `RegionSim` methods, and the effects show up as numbers.
- **The world doesn't talk back.** Events (`REGION_EVENT_DEFS`, `historical.ts`) are one-shot modifiers with no choices. News is a flat 40-line log. Rivals don't react to *you* in a legible way.
- **The UI is a wall of panels.** It's one huge `regionview.ts` with no command layer and no onboarding.

The audit says you can't lose, the exploits are uncapped, and combat never changes the war score.

The goal is PaxHistoria's feel without a live LLM: a *reactive, narrated* world built from deterministic systems and data-driven content. The framework we keep:
- the headless seeded sim
- the save system
- the rival AI and diplomacy
- the economy

## Design pillars
1. **Every action creates a visible reaction within 1–3 turns.** That reaction comes from a named actor: a rival leader, a faction, a Notable, or the press.
2. **Diegetic narration, not LLM.** Text is generated by grammar and templates, drawing on actors' personality, relations, memory and era vocabulary.
3. **Fewer, heavier decisions.** Move from a 30-minute tick to a monthly "turn" with an agenda of 2–5 decisions. Auto-pause on reactions.
4. **Stakes.** You can lose, crises escalate, and wars resolve through the map.

## Core new systems (sim, headless)
- **A. Command layer** (`src/sim/commands.ts`)
  - Every player act becomes a serializable `Command {type, args, day}`, applied by a single `applyCommand`.
  - This enables replay, undo-preview, AI parity, and "the world remembers what you did".
  - The UI stops calling `RegionSim` methods directly.
- **B. Memory & reputation** (`src/sim/memory.ts`)
  - Each actor keeps a decaying ledger of tagged deeds: `betrayed_treaty`, `printed_money`, `crushed_strike`, `annexed_x`, and so on.
  - Rival AI weights and faction loyalty read from the ledger.
  - Reputation axes: trustworthy, aggressive, reformist, pious/secular (era-flavoured).
- **C. Reaction engine** (`src/sim/reactions.ts`)
  - Data-driven rules: `on <deed|state predicate> → actors respond with <stance/diplomatic act/event>`, weighted by personality.
  - This is the core of the "world reacts" feel, and it is fully deterministic from the `auxRng` random stream.
- **D. Event/decision chains in JSON** (`src/data/events/*.json`)
  - Includes a small trigger DSL (predicates over sim state and memory) and options with effects and follow-ups.
  - Notables voice them (GDD §8.1).
  - This replaces the hard-coded `REGION_EVENT_DEFS`, which get migrated.
- **E. Press & diplomacy text generator** (`src/sim/narrative/`)
  - A Tracery-style grammar, written in-house (no dependency).
  - Produces headlines by era (1919 broadsheet → 2080 feed), leader statements and cables.
  - Output has a structured tone: angry, conciliatory, threat.
  - Replaces `addLog` with `NewsItem {actor, deedRef, headline, body, severity}`.
- **F. Stakes pass**
  - Unrest pressure is on by default, and exploit multipliers get capped.
  - Battle outcomes feed war score and occupation, using the existing army-group and occupation save fields.

- **G. Living map & dynamic borders** (`src/sim/territory.ts`)
  - **Hex ownership.** Every hex gets an `owner` and a `control` value (0–1), instead of borders being fixed per province.
    - Control spreads each month from the nearest seats of power: capital, towns, forts and garrisons.
    - It is weighted by distance, terrain, roads and loyalty.
    - Borders are the edges where the highest-control owner changes. They drift, bulge and erode over time rather than snapping.
  - **What pushes borders.** Player input moves them through:
    - founding towns and building roads/forts, which extend reach
    - war: occupation flips control at the front line, and peace deals cede whole zones
    - diplomacy: purchase, a province swap, a vassal's borders merging into yours
    - collapse: when unrest is high, hexes break away into a rebel or successor polity
    - migration and culture pressure
  - **Contested and disputed zones.** Hexes that two claimants both hold at control ≥ 0.3 become contested. They are drawn hatched and generate claims that the reaction engine uses (C).
  - **Claims & grievances.** Losing territory creates a revanchist claim in the loser's memory (B). This drives later wars and rhetoric in the news.
  - **Emergent polities.**
    - Secession, civil war splits and colonial independence spawn new nations mid-game, generated with system H.
    - Mergers (unions, conquest) remove nations.
  - **Map rendering.**
    - Borders are drawn as smoothed polylines from the hex edges.
    - Animated border shifts are replayed on the month change.
    - A front-line overlay, and zones where control is fading.
    - A "History scrubber" replays border snapshots by year. This needs one compact snapshot per year, which is a save schema change.
    - Map names are generated (H), and cities are renamed after conquest ("Ostmark → Neu-Ostmark").
- **H. Procedural rival nations** (`src/sim/procgen/nation.ts`, seeded)
  - **Identity.**
    - A culture-family seed drives syllable tables that produce the nation's name, cities and people's names.
    - Nation names include forms such as "Republic of Varnesia" and "Holy Dominion of Kest".
    - Leaders are generated as Notables: name, age, traits, ideology, and a succession by regime type. When the leader dies, the nation's personality partly shifts.
  - **Flags.**
    - Built from grammar-based heraldry: a division (tricolor, cross, canton, quartered), a charge (star, emblem, sun, beast silhouette), and a palette drawn from the culture and ideology.
    - Rendered as vector graphics on the canvas and cached.
    - Flags change with revolutions: communist red with an emblem, restored monarchy with the crown added, and so on.
  - **National personality.** Weighted on the existing `RivalNation` personality weights:
    - ambition, honor/grudge-holding, ideology axis, risk appetite, trade vs. war bias, and pettiness toward the player
    - plus 1–2 signature "quirks" from a table (e.g. "never forgives naval humiliation", "courts every new republic")
    - These feed the rival AI, the reaction engine and the voice of generated text (formal / bombastic / terse).
  - **Mode choice.** `rival_nations.json` becomes an optional "historical preset" mode. Procgen is the default for new games. Each game seed produces a unique, reproducible world.

## UI/UX rework (`src/ui/`)
- **Layout**
  - A "Newspaper / Dispatch" feed replaces the log. It's the main screen and the reaction surface.
  - A Turn agenda bar: pending decisions, advisor recommendations, "end month".
  - A map-first layout with a contextual inspector (one panel, tabbed) instead of about 9 draggable windows.
  - A diplomacy screen shows each rival's portrait, stance, what they remember about you, and a relation trend.
  - Map overlays: relations heat, unrest, trade flow, war fronts.
- **Tooltips:** causal "why" tooltips. For example: "Relations −12: you printed money (−5), annexed Ostmark (−7)".
- **Onboarding:** a scenario-based first 20 years with guided decisions.
- **Accessibility:** an options menu, a colorblind palette, key remapping.
- **Refactor:** split `regionview.ts` into a view module per screen as part of the rework (edit and extract, not rewrite).

## Phasing (each phase shippable)
1. **Foundations:** the command layer, the `Math.random` determinism fix (`region.ts:~10381`), and the stakes pass (F).
2. **Procgen nations (H) + hex control & dynamic borders (G core)**, including the border rendering.
3. **Memory + reaction engine,** with about 30 reaction rules, plus claims/grievances from border changes.
4. **News feed + narrative generator + turn agenda UI.** This is the first point where the "feel" becomes playable.
5. **JSON event chains + trigger DSL,** plus secession and emergent polities.
6. **UI overhaul:** inspector, diplomacy screen, overlays, history scrubber, onboarding, options and accessibility.
7. **Content breadth:** eras 7–8, more culture families and more flag charges.

## Hard stops (need approval per CLAUDE.md)
- Save schema changes in phases 1, 2 and 4. New fields: command log, memory ledgers, event-chain state. Each requires a `SAVE_SCHEMA_VERSION` bump.
- Restructuring the events and tick cadence (the monthly turn) touches core sim systems.
- No new dependencies: the grammar engine and DSL are written in-house.

## Verification (per phase)
- `npm run build`, `npm test`
- Determinism test: the same seed plus the same command log gives the same save.
- `npm run sim -- 80 20`: check loss rate > 0 on default settings, and check reaction frequency and news volume per year.
- Procgen: 100 seeds give valid, unique names and flags, and personality distributions stay in range.
- Borders: across 80-year runs, borders change on average without flicker (control hysteresis), and every nation keeps a connected core.
- Manual playtest: every agenda decision produces a visible named reaction within 3 months.
