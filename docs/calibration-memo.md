# Calibration Memo — Gameplay-Visible Simulation Constants vs. Real-World Data

**Date:** 2026-07-04 · **Scope:** the ~15 most gameplay-visible constants in `src/sim/systems/monetary.ts`, `demographics.ts`, `climate.ts`, `loans.ts`/`lenders.ts`, `elections.ts`, plus the tax bounds in `region.ts`. Constants are judged against the game's full **1919–2100** span (policy rates hit 19–20% in 1980–81; world birth rates were ~35/1000 in the interwar era), not just today's values.

This memo is **evidence only** — no source file was changed. Verdicts: **IN RANGE** (matches real data within its era envelope), **DEFENSIBLE STYLIZATION** (deliberately compressed for pacing; sign and order of magnitude right), **OUT OF RANGE** (sign or order of magnitude misleads; candidate for an orchestrator-approved change).

---

## 0. Time-base caveat (read first — it governs every "monthly" conversion)

The game year is **60 sim-days** (`DAYS_PER_YEAR = 60`, `src/sim/defs.ts:442`; a calendar "month" is 5 days, `defs.ts:444`). But `monthlyUpdate()` fires every **30 sim-days** (`region.ts:6446`), i.e. **2 "monthly" ticks per game-year** — the climate code knows this (`WARMING_LAG_TICKS` comment: "two ticks a game-year", `region.ts:3225-3228`), but the monetary/demographic code annualizes ×12 (e.g. `region.ts:10131`: `annualRevenue = gdpLastMonth × taxRate × 12`).

Consequence: any flow documented as "X/month (~12X/yr)" is actually delivered at **2X per game-year** — a **6× compression** of all monthly-rate flows relative to their own annualized comments. Where this matters per-constant it is called out below; conversions in the tables use the **nominal** interpretation (tick = 1 calendar month, ×12 = annual) because that is what the code comments and UI assume, with the delivered-rate divergence noted.

## Cross-cutting findings

1. **Tick-cadence unit drift (§0)** is the single largest realism distortion: every "monthly" monetary/demographic flow is delivered at 1/6 of its own documented annualized rate, while the climate system is correctly calibrated to the true 2-ticks/game-year cadence. Nothing needs to change numerically *if* this is documented as the intended 6× time compression — but two comments (`monetary.ts:44`, the print "~12%/yr" gloss; `region.ts:10131`'s ×12 annualization) actively mislead future tuners.
2. Where constants were checked against a named episode (oil-shock +4.5pp vs. 1973's +9pp; ECS 3.25 vs. AR6 3.0; DSR 0.18 vs. BIS; CBR/CDR vs. UN WPP), they hold up well — the "design-intent citation" comments are mostly *better* than they claim.
3. The two OUT OF RANGE findings share a signature: a headline mechanic (print regime, aging crisis) whose narrative fires but whose delivered number is ~10–20× too small to be felt. Both are one-line, clamp-bounded fixes.

## Top 5 highest-value corrections (ranked by realism-per-risk)

1. **Print-regime inflation lift +0.010 → +0.08–0.10** (`monetary.ts:46`). Makes the print regime deliver the 10–12%/yr inflation its own comment promises (steady state 0.10–0.12, safely under the 0.50 clamp and the envelope test's 0.30 bound). One constant; bounded by existing clamps; biggest gameplay-truth gain.
2. **Aging-crisis pension burden: drop the `/12`** (`demographics.ts:53`), delivering ~1.8% of annual GDP (~15% of a 10% tax take) instead of 0.125%. Pure treasury sink — no feedback loops touched — and turns a dead log line into the fiscal squeeze OECD projections describe.
3. **Document the 2-ticks-per-game-year cadence** in `monetary.ts`/`demographics.ts` headers and correct the "~12%/yr" and `×12` annualization comments (`monetary.ts:44`, `region.ts:10131`). Zero code risk; prevents every future mis-tuning of "monthly" constants.
4. **`MAX_POLICY_RATE` 0.15 → 0.20** (`region.ts:1824`) so a Volcker-scale disinflation is playable against a 30–50% inflation spiral. Low risk, but note the interaction: bondRate = policyRate + up to 0.25 spread could then reach 0.45, above the envelope test's empirical `RATE_MAX = 0.30` — the envelope comment already anticipates this; re-run the 20-seed sweep before accepting.
5. **Steepen the world-emissions time profile** (`region.ts:5096-5097`): lower the pre-1950 ramp and raise the 1950–2030 plateau so ppm/yr tracks 0.3 → 0.8 → 2.6 (NOAA) instead of a flat ~1.2. Moderate risk (shifts the 2040 branch-year warming and the ghost-line projection); highest fidelity gain on the game's marquee system, so worth a `npm run sim` A/B before committing.
