# Centuria Lore Bible

Canon for all flavor content (names, notable bios, diplomacy text, historical beats).
Rule zero, from the GDD: **rhyme with history, don't recite it.** Nothing in Centuria names
a real nation, person, or battle. Events should feel like plausible cousins of the 20th
century — a Great War just ended in 1919, a credit mania will someday break, oil will matter,
the climate bill comes due — but always at one remove, with invented proper nouns.

## Tone guide

- Register: telegraphic frontier-newspaper prose. Concrete nouns, active verbs, no fantasy
  diction, no modern slang. "The granary swells," not "food +12."
- Log lines are one or two sentences. They name a person or a place before a number.
- Irony is allowed; cynicism is earned. The world is hard but not hopeless.
- The player's nation is never named by the lore — it is "the State," "the Republic," or
  whatever the player proclaims. Rivals refer to it obliquely ("the river upstarts,"
  "the young state").
- Dates anchor everything: beats should read like entries in a century-long chronicle.

## The world of 1919

A continent exhausted by the Great War (1914–1918 analog, never named beyond "the Great
War"). Old empires cracked; new republics, councils, and juntas scramble in the wreckage.
The player's region is a fringe territory the exhausted powers ignored — which is exactly
why it can rise.

## The ten rival nations

Each entry: canon paragraph, then a **name flavor** tag used by the name-pool data (L2) and
a **voice** note used by diplomacy/war text (L5).

### Vasterholm — the wounded hegemon
Defeated in the Great War and stripped of its river provinces, Vasterholm rebuilt itself
around grievance. Chancellor Aldric von Stahl's one-party federation runs on steel quotas,
veterans' leagues, and the promise that the rivers will come home. Its diplomats are
correct, cold, and keep meticulous lists. **Name flavor:** hard Germanic — Aldric, Dietmar,
Greta, Wolfram; surnames Stahl, Brandt, Krieger, Falkenrath. **Voice:** clipped ultimatums;
speaks of "restoration," "order," and debts of honor.

### Kalimera — the sunlit ledger
A coastal republic that sat out the worst of the war and carried everyone's cargo at
everyone's expense. President Maren Costa's merchant houses believe every problem is a
price not yet agreed. **Name flavor:** Mediterranean — Maren, Luca, Bianca, Rafael;
surnames Costa, Marino, Ferro, Albanesi. **Voice:** warm, transactional; every threat is
phrased as a regrettable tariff.

### Tyrennia — the burning creed
The war convinced Tyrennia's prelates that the old order died for its sins. Supreme Prelate
Oswyn Brennan exports doctrine the way others export coal — through missions, pamphlets,
and the occasional zealous mob. **Name flavor:** Celtic-ecclesiastic — Oswyn, Deirdre,
Caleb, Rhiannon; surnames Brennan, Carrick, Whitefriar, Malone. **Voice:** sermonic;
condemns, absolves, and never merely disagrees.

### Karelia — the closed passes
Mountain Karelia survived the war by letting no army through and no promise out.
Minister-President Torsten Skau's constitutional court of clan elders counts grudges the
way banks count coupons. **Name flavor:** Nordic-Finnic — Torsten, Aino, Eero, Sigrid;
surnames Skau, Kivi, Halonen, Berg. **Voice:** terse, suspicious; long memory, short
sentences.

### The Sundered Communes — the rotating chair
Born of wartime mutinies, the Communes are a federation of workers' councils that found
revolution pays best when sold to both sides. The Architect-Council chair rotates; the
ledgers do not. **Name flavor:** Slavic — Alexi, Vanya, Irina, Bogdan; surnames Petrov,
Sokolova, Marek, Volkov. **Voice:** ideological garnish over mercenary terms; comrades
until the invoice.

### The Northern League — the star and the sail
A confederation of harbor cities whose merchant fleet came through the war unscorched while
its rivals' navies burned. Admiral Soren the Navigator holds the League together with
charts, convoy schedules, and shared lighthouse dues. **Name flavor:** Scandinavian
maritime — Soren, Njall, Astrid, Leif; surnames Havik, Strand, Norling, Vinter. **Voice:**
bluff, practical; measures friendship in safe anchorages.

### The Highland Federation — the heather charter
Clans that traded just enough autonomy for a common defense pact and swore to trade no
more. High Chief Grania Mara's federation is poor in treasuries and rich in cousins.
**Name flavor:** Gaelic — Grania, Ewan, Moira, Callum; surnames MacRae, Dunmore, Craigie,
Bhaltair. **Voice:** proud, formulaic courtesy; insults are remembered for generations.

### The Crescent Sultanate — the lamp and the derrick
Sultan Jahangir al-Rashid rules where the caravan roads cross the new oilfields, balancing
scholars, pilgrims, and drilling concessions. The great powers covet the sands; the
Sultanate sells them survey maps with the good parts missing. **Name flavor:**
Arabic-Persian — Jahangir, Layla, Tariq, Soraya; surnames al-Rashid, Qadiri, Farouk,
ibn Sallum. **Voice:** courtly, layered; a refusal arrives wrapped as a compliment.

### The Iron Republics — the hammer concordat
City-states fused by wartime production boards into one industrial junta. Chief Magistrate
Petra Eisenmann's magistracy publishes output figures the way churches publish scripture.
**Name flavor:** industrial-Germanic — Petra, Konrad, Use, Emil; surnames Eisenmann,
Schacht, Werfel, Gruber. **Voice:** statistical; grievances are itemized, wars are
"corrections."

### The Sunset Empire — the long dusk
An empire that won its last war a century ago and has been losing the peace ever since.
Emperor Marcos the Eternal presides over gilded ministries, restless provinces, and
reformers who whisper in antique dialects. **Name flavor:** Latin-Iberian imperial —
Marcos, Lucia, Esteban, Beatriz; surnames Herrera, Valcázar, Montiel, Duarte. **Voice:**
grandiloquent nostalgia; every demand cites a treaty older than its signatories.

## Notable archetypes (for bio beats, L3)

Six roles exist: Mayor, Doctor, Captain, Granger, Forewoman, Reeve. Beats should be gated
by traits where the trait plainly enables or forbids the story (a scandal needs a flaw; a
heroic slog needs grit). Three to four sketches per role:

- **Mayor** — *the Fixer* (settles feuds, trades favors, knows every ledger); *the Orator*
  (speeches on the courthouse steps, better at hope than arithmetic); *the Grafter*
  (skims the road fund, buys rounds at the tavern — scandal fodder); *the Builder*
  (obsessed with one great public work).
- **Doctor** — *the Circuit Rider* (vaccinates the outlying farms, sleeps in barns);
  *the Modernist* (orders equipment from catalogues, feuds with midwives); *the Haunted
  Veteran* (learned surgery at the front, drinks to forget it); *the Quack-adjacent*
  (tonics of dubious provenance — scandal fodder).
- **Captain** — *the Drillmaster* (militia on the green till dusk); *the Old Campaigner*
  (war stories that grow a foot each winter); *the Martinet* (order at the cost of love —
  grievance beats); *the Reluctant Hero* (hates the work, does it anyway).
- **Granger** — *the Prize-Grower* (county fairs, winter wheat); *the Land-Hungry*
  (buys every failing farm, tenants grumble); *the Almanac Sage* (reads weather in bird
  flight, right often enough); *the Cooperative Organizer* (grain pools, shared silos).
- **Forewoman** — *the Timber Boss* (fells the deadfall, respected and feared); *the Safety
  Crusader* (new rules after the accident); *the Speed Artist* (records fall, corners get
  cut — scandal/accident fodder); *the Union Voice* (speaks for the crews to the owners).
- **Reeve** — *the Booster* (glowing letters printed back east); *the Meticulous Clerk*
  (finds the missing shillings, makes enemies); *the Social Climber* (dines with anyone
  titled); *the Quiet Fixer* (paperwork vanishes, problems with it — scandal fodder).

Trait gating: a beat that alleges corruption, laziness, or recklessness must require a
matching flaw trait; a beat of exceptional diligence, endurance, or cheer must require the
matching virtue trait. Neutral civic beats stay ungated.
